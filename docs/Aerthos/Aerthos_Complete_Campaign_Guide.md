# AERTHOS: A Complete Campaign Guide
## *A Fragmented Realm of Ancient Powers, Buried Secrets, and Old-School Adventure*

---

## I. THE WORLD AT A GLANCE

Aerthos is a realm where the fabric of reality itself is woven from the Nine-Point Alignment system—not merely a philosophical guide for mortals, but a tangible cosmic force. Celestial beings embody Law and Chaos, Good and Evil, constantly influencing events from their distant planes. The Blood War between Demons (Tanar'ri) and Devils (Baatezu) occasionally spills into the mortal realm, seeking proxies and spreading corruption. True Neutrality is a powerful, if rare, state, often associated with ancient druidic orders or enigmatic planar travelers.

This is a world of resource management, deadly wilderness, perilous dungeons, and forgotten empires—a realm built for classic AD&D 1st Edition adventure.

---

## II. COSMOLOGY & PLANAR STRUCTURE

### The Nature of Magic
Magic in Aerthos is **potent and perilous**. Spells require strict adherence to material, verbal, and somatic components. High-level magic is rare and feared—archmages are legends, not common NPCs. Most mages are reclusive scholars who guard their spellbooks with lethal determination.

### Planar Rifts & Extra-Planar Threats
The barriers between Aerthos and other planes are **thin in certain locations**, particularly in ancient ruins or areas of intense magical confluence. This means encounters with:
- **Elementals** (especially in volcanic or storm-wracked regions)
- **Genies** (in desert ruins or beneath mountain peaks)
- **Lower-level demons and devils** (in cursed lands or corrupted temples)

These rifts are not random—they are scars from the past, maintained by forgotten rituals or sustained by ongoing conflict.

### Ancient Artifacts & Cursed Relics
Aerthos is littered with remnants of past ages—powerful magic items, cursed relics, and unique artifacts that can shift the balance of power. These are rarely found in pristine condition and often carry their own dangers, demands, or sentient wills.

## III. GEOGRAPHY & CLIMATE

### Climate Consistency (DMG p. 87)
*The climate of Aerthos need not follow Earth's laws, but it must be internally consistent. Large predators cannot exist without a sufficient food source.*

---

### **The Shattered Peaks** (Central Mountain Range)

**Climate:**
- The peaks are so high they **pierce the atmosphere's "blanket"** (DMG p. 87). The air is thin and bitterly cold—torches burn at half-brightness above 10,000 feet.
- However, **thermal vents** from the deep-dwarven forges create artificial warm pockets. The violent interaction between cold mountain air and scalding steam creates the perpetual magical storms known as **"The Anvil's Breath"**—violet lightning and howling winds that make high-altitude travel lethal.

**Ecology:**
- **The Predator/Prey Problem:** Hill Giants and Griffons dominate these peaks. How do they survive without constantly raiding settlements?
- **The Solution:** A rapid-growth food source called **"Rock-Moss"**—a purple, lichen-like tuber that grows overnight on rocky outcrops. Massive herds of **Mountain Goats** and **Giant Marmots** feed on this, breeding prolifically. This explains the Giants' survival (though they still prefer the taste of humans).

**Inhabitants:**
- **The Ironfast Dwarves:** Residing deep within the uncorrupted fortresses. They follow a **Geriatocracy** (DMG p. 89)—only dwarves over 300 years old sit on the "Iron Council." Younger dwarves, regardless of wealth or prowess, have no political voice until they "gray."
- **Hill Giants:** Herding their goat flocks on high plateaus.
- **Griffons & Rocs:** Nesting in the highest peaks.
- **Duergar & Worse:** Dwelling in the Underdark below.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- Dwarven ruins overtaken by duergar slavers.
- A crashed airship (or giant's treasure hoard) high in the storm-wracked passes.
- A legendary forge-temple where weapons can be enchanted—if you survive the journey.

---

### **The Whispering Marshes** (Western Swamplands)

**Climate:**
- The Marshes suffer from a permanent **"Greenhouse Effect"** (DMG p. 87). Heavy, magical cloud cover traps heat rising from the swamp's decomposition. Even at night, temperatures rarely drop below 80°F.
- **Visibility is reduced to 30 yards** by perpetual warm fog.

**Ecology:**
- The heat and decay support **Giant Lizards, Hydras, Shambling Mounds,** and microbial oozes.
- **Lizardfolk tribes** worship the "Greenhouse" heat as a divine force.
- Ancient ruins slowly sink into the muck, home to **Hags, Will-o-Wisps,** and forgotten druid circles.

**Inhabitants:**
- **Neutral-aligned Fey creatures** guard hidden groves.
- **Lizardfolk Shamans** perform blood rituals at submerged stone altars.
- **Black Dragons** lair in the deepest, most inaccessible bogs.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- A missing expedition sought a sunken temple of an elder god.
- A hag coven offers a dark bargain to desperate travelers.
- A merchant hires the party to recover valuable alchemical herbs—only found in the heart of the swamp.

---

### **The Verdant Heartlands** (Central Plains & Forests)

**Climate:**
- Temperate, with four seasons. The most habitable region.
- **Terrain:** Plains/Light Forest. Movement cost: 1x on roads, 1.5x off-road.

**Ecology:**
- The most populated region, featuring rolling hills, fertile plains, and dense ancient forests.
- **Dangerous Wilderness:** Random encounter tables here are long and varied. Even a simple journey can turn deadly (1-in-6 chance of patrols or bandits per day on roads, higher off-road).

**Inhabitants:**
- **The Free Cities of Eldoria:** Human city-states following a **Syndicracy** (DMG p. 89)—government by business guilds.
- **The Silvan Concordance:** Elven kingdoms hidden in the oldest forests. Aloof, isolationist, fiercely protective (Chaotic Good / Neutral Good).
- **Halfling shires, human farmsteads, and isolated wizard towers.**

**Hidden Dangers:**
- Ancient **barrow mounds** hide undead and cursed treasures.
- Secluded groves are home to **chaotic fey** or even a solitary dragon.
- Bandits, cultists, and owlbears roam the woods.

---

### **The Sunken Coast** (Eastern Archipelago)

**Climate:**
- Warm, humid, with unpredictable tides and violent storms.

**Ecology:**
- An ancient cataclysm caused large parts of the coastline to sink beneath the waves.
- Now, treacherous **archipelagos and submerged cities** dot the waters.
- **Merfolk, Sahuagin, and abyssal aberrations** rule these depths.

**Inhabitants:**
- **Merfolk kingdoms** (neutral, wary of surface-dwellers).
- **Sahuagin warbands** (raiders who attack coastal settlements).
- **Pirates and smugglers** use hidden coves as bases.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- Shipwrecks promise treasure—and doom.
- Tides reveal ancient land bridges to treasure-filled ruins for only an hour.
- A cult seeks to awaken something terrible in the drowned city of Ys'Thara.

---

### **The Scarred Wastes** (Southern Desolation)

**Climate:**
- Volcanic badlands, perpetually blighted by a curse from a long-dead sorcerer-king.
- Ash-gray soil, tainted water sources (poison save required to drink).

**Ecology:**
- Scarce resources mean constant tribal warfare.
- **Orc tribes, Gnolls, and savage humanoids** roam the wastes.
- Ancient, blasted fortresses stand as monuments to a forgotten age, guarded by **elementals or constructs**.

**Inhabitants:**
- **Orc tribes** ride giant vultures for scouting and raids.
- **Gnoll warbands** worship Yeenoghu in blood-soaked shrines.
- **Fire giants** in obsidian fortresses deep in the volcanic heart.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- A caravan needs protection crossing the wastes.
- Ancient relics of the sorcerer-king lie in the ruins—cursed and powerful.
- A tribe of orcs offers a truce to fight a greater threat (fire giants or elemental cultists).

## IV. POLITICAL LANDSCAPE & FACTIONS

### **The Free Cities of Eldoria** (Lawful Neutral / Chaotic Neutral)

**Government:** **Syndicracy** (DMG p. 89)—rule by business guilds.

**Structure:**
- **Upper Class (The Syndics):** Guildmasters of the Iron, Grain, and Cloth guilds. Only they can vote for the "High Syndic" (the ruler, called a **Doge** or **Burgomaster**).
- **Middle Class:** Master artisans, mercenaries with property >100 acres (retired adventurers), lesser priests.
- **Lower Class:** Laborers, apprentices, "Freemen" (starting adventurers).

**Taxes & Economics:**
- **Property Tax:** 20 gold pieces annually on owned property (safehouses, shops), or face confiscation (DMG p. 88).
- **Currency Exchange:** Ancient dungeon coins are not accepted. The "Street of Money Changers" charges a **10% tax** to convert ancient gold into stamped Eldorian Crowns (DMG p. 90).

**The Cities:**
- **Eldoria (The Capital):** A massive walled city at the confluence of two trade rivers. A hub of commerce, intrigue, and corruption.
- **Oakhaven (Border Town):** The gateway to the Shattered Peaks. Adventurer economy causes **inflation**—a torch costs 1 silver piece here (10x normal) because adventurers will pay it.
  - **Gate Toll:** 5 coppers for non-citizens (DMG p. 90).
  - **Typical Inhabitants (DMG p. 88):** Mostly "Laboring Males/Females" (HP 2-7, 0 Level). If the party starts a brawl, the town militia (1st level fighters) will be summoned.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- Guild politics: rival factions hire the party for espionage or sabotage.
- A corrupt Syndic needs to be exposed (or protected).
- The party is hired to guard a caravan or investigate a missing shipment.

---

### **The Silvan Concordance** (Chaotic Good / Neutral Good)

**Government:** Ancient monarchies, ruled by timeless elf-lords.

**Structure:**
- Elves view shorter-lived races with disdain or detached curiosity.
- They guard ancient magical secrets and are fiercely protective of their forest domains.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- The elves need mortal aid to deal with a threat they cannot ignore (corrupted treants, a planar rift, a dragon).
- A stolen artifact must be returned to the Concordance—or kept from them.
- The party stumbles into elven lands and must navigate deadly politics and ancient laws.

---

### **The Ironfast Dwarves** (Lawful Good / Lawful Neutral)

**Government:** **Geriatocracy**—only dwarves over 300 years old govern (DMG p. 89).

**Structure:**
- Proud, stubborn, skilled artisans.
- Their numbers dwindle due to ceaseless wars with goblins, duergar, and worse.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- A dwarven hold is besieged—hire adventurers to break the siege or retrieve a lost relic.
- The party discovers a lost dwarven forge-temple with legendary crafting potential.
- Political tensions: younger dwarves consider rebellion against the "Iron Council."

---

### **The Cult of the Serpent Eye** (Chaotic Evil)

**Structure:**
- Worships an ancient, extra-planar entity of pure chaos and evil.
- Operates in the shadows of every major city.

**Goals:**
- Undermine order, spread discord, and eventually unleash their dark patron upon Aerthos.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- Investigate mysterious disappearances tied to serpent iconography.
- Infiltrate a cult ritual to prevent a summoning.
- A noble or merchant is secretly a cult leader—expose or eliminate them.

---

### **The Grey Wardens** (True Neutral / Neutral Good)

**Structure:**
- A scattered order of monster hunters, scholars, and wilderness guides.
- Dedicated to understanding and combating the myriad threats that plague Aerthos.

**Philosophy:**
- Value knowledge and survival above strict dogma.
- Often neutral arbiters in conflicts between factions.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- Recruit the party for a monster hunt or scholarly expedition.
- Provide lore, maps, and quest hooks.
- A Warden has gone rogue—track them down.

## V. STARTING CAMPAIGN AREA (DMG pp. 91-99)

*The PDF suggests starting small: a settlement, a background, and a nearby ruin.*

---

### **The Settlement: Oakhaven**

**Location:** Nestled at the foot of the Shattered Peaks, where the Heartlands meet the mountains.

**Description:**
- A fortified wooden palisade town.
- The only safe place to rest and buy supplies before venturing into the mountains.

**Economy:**
- **High inflation** due to adventurer demand.
- A standard torch costs **1 silver piece** (10x normal).
- **Gate Toll:** 5 coppers for non-citizens.
- **Currency Exchange:** Ancient coins must be exchanged at a 10% tax on the "Street of Money Changers."

**Inhabitants:**
- Mostly "Laboring Males/Females" (HP 2-7, 0 Level)—farmers, miners, tavern staff. They are **not** fighters.
- **Town Militia:** 1st level fighters, summoned if the party causes trouble.

**Key NPCs:**

#### **Silas the "Merchant"** (Rival NPC)
- **Race:** Human
- **Class:** Fighter (Retired)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Evil (uses the law to exploit)
- **Appearance (DMG p. 100):**
  - *Age:* Mature (5)
  - *General:* Immaculate (4)—rare for this border region.
- **Sanity:** Very Stable (1)
- **Possessions:** Superabundant (0)—owns the only equipment shop in Oakhaven.
- **Tendencies (DMG p. 100):**
  - *Suspicious/Cautious (12)*
  - *Precise/Exacting (13)*
- **Role:** Silas buys loot from adventurers but offers only **40% value**, citing "taxes." If players argue, he uses his "Precise" nature to find flaws in the goods. He is a primary antagonist through economics, not combat.

#### **The Guide** (Quest Giver)
- **Race:** Human
- **Class:** Thief (3rd Level)
- **Alignment:** Neutral
- **Role:** Tells the party of the **Ruined Keep of Kaldor** (for a fee or a share of the loot).

---

### **The Dungeon: The Ruined Keep of Kaldor**

**Location:** 6 miles (1 hex, 24-mile scale) north of Oakhaven, in the foothills.

**Background (DMG p. 91):**
- The Keep was abandoned by humans due to a plague.
- A tribe of **Goblins** has moved in—not as random monsters, but as **refugees** driven from the Shattered Peaks by Ogres.

**Monster Placement Logic (DMG p. 91):**
- The Goblins are desperate and dangerous.
- They utilize the Keep's cellars to grow mushrooms (food source—ecology!).

**Treasure Placement (DMG p. 92):**
- The Goblins do **not** have chests of gold.
- **Loot:** Stolen goods—bolts of silk, a cask of fine wine (worth 20gp), a single **silver necklace** hidden in a rotting mattress (investigation required).
- **Magic Items (DMG p. 93):** Do not place powerful items in starting dungeons. The Goblin leader has a **Dagger +1** that he uses to bully the others.

**Adventure Hooks:**
- The Goblins are raiding caravans for food, not gold.
- Oakhaven's militia is too weak to deal with them—hire adventurers.
- Rumor: The Keep's deepest cellar connects to the Underdark.

## VI. CAMPAIGN MECHANICS & AD&D FLAVOR

### Resource Management is Key
- **Encumbrance rules matter!** Players must carefully consider what equipment they carry.
- **Supplies are vital:** Rations, torches, rope, waterskins, and healing potions are more important than gold.

### Dangerous Wilderness
- **Travel is not safe.** Random encounter tables for each region are long and varied.
- Even a simple journey can become life-or-death.
- **Example:** Traveling from Oakhaven to the Keep of Kaldor (6 miles) has a **1-in-6 chance** of encountering goblins, wolves, or worse.

### Dungeon Delving Focus
- Adventures involve exploring multi-level dungeons filled with traps, puzzles, and diverse monsters.
- **Careful mapping is essential.**
- **Light sources matter:** Torches last 6 turns, lanterns last longer but require oil.

### Alignment Matters
- NPCs react differently based on the characters' visible alignments.
- **Good-aligned characters** find aid in temples.
- **Evil characters** might be sought by nefarious patrons—or hunted by paladins.

### Mysteries and Lore
- The world is old, filled with forgotten empires, ancient evils, and lost histories.
- Discovering these secrets is a core part of the adventure.
- **Example:** Who was the sorcerer-king who cursed the Scarred Wastes? Can the curse be lifted?

## IX. CAMPAIGN HOOKS & ADVENTURE SEEDS

### **Tier 1 (Levels 1-3): Local Troubles**
1. **The Goblin Problem:** Clear the Keep of Kaldor (as described above).
2. **The Missing Caravan:** A merchant hires the party to find a lost caravan. Trail leads to the marshes (lizardfolk or hags).
3. **Cult in the Sewers:** Disappearances in Oakhaven lead to a Serpent Eye cult hideout beneath the town.
4. **Silas's Scheme:** The merchant is selling cursed items. Investigate and stop him (or join him?).

### **Tier 2 (Levels 4-7): Regional Threats**
1. **The Dwarven Siege:** The Ironfast Dwarves hire the party to break a duergar siege on their hold.
2. **The Sunken Temple:** An elven patron seeks a lost artifact in the Sunken Coast ruins.
3. **The Orc Warlord:** Orc tribes are uniting in the Scarred Wastes. Stop the warlord before they invade the Heartlands.
4. **The Planar Rift:** A rift to the Elemental Plane of Fire is opening in the mountains. Close it before fire elementals overrun the region.

### **Tier 3 (Levels 8-12): World-Shaking Events**
1. **The Serpent's Awakening:** The Cult of the Serpent Eye is summoning their patron. Stop the ritual or face an apocalypse.
2. **The Sorcerer-King's Curse:** Lift the curse on the Scarred Wastes by destroying the lich-king who created it.
3. **The Blood War Spills Over:** Demons and devils are fighting on the mortal plane. Choose a side—or banish both.
4. **The Dragon's Ultimatum:** An ancient red dragon demands tribute from Eldoria. Negotiate, fight, or find another solution.

## XI. CONCLUSION: THE PHILOSOPHY OF AERTHOS

Aerthos is a world built on the principles of **classic AD&D 1st Edition**:
- **Challenge over power fantasy:** Players earn their victories.
- **Resources matter:** Every torch, every ration, every healing potion counts.
- **The world is dangerous:** Even travel between towns is a risk.
- **Ecology and logic:** Monsters exist for reasons, not just as XP bags.
- **Politics and intrigue:** Not every problem is solved with a sword.
- **Ancient mysteries:** The world is old, and its secrets are buried deep.

This is a world where **alignment matters**, where **factions clash**, where **magic is rare and feared**, and where **adventurers are desperate, greedy, or heroic—never mundane.**

Welcome to Aerthos, brave adventurer. May your dice always roll high.
